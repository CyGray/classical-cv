Subject: Correction to Robustness Test Protocol (Data Leakage) — diagrams attached

Dr. Oh,

Following up on our session — attaching diagrams to make the protocol change and the numbers clearer than I managed to explain live.

What changed: the robustness test previously trained LBPH on one photo, then used a corrupted copy of that SAME photo as the probe. The model was matching a distorted copy of data it had already memorized, not an unseen photo. This produced an inflated result (LBPH clean acceptance 100%, 86.66% averaged across the 41 modifications) that measured memorization, not recognition.

Fix: the test set is now restricted to identities with at least 2 real photos (1,680 such identities in LFW). One photo enrolls the gallery; a genuinely different second photo of the same person is corrupted and used as the probe. This is a real test of generalization to an unseen photo.

Under the corrected protocol, LBPH's real performance is much lower: clean TAR 2.26%, dropping to 0.93% at the heaviest modification tier, ~1.4% averaged across all 41 modifications, at the deployed threshold.

Before you ask — I checked whether this is just an overly strict threshold. It isn't: even loosening the threshold to accept 10% of impostors (unusable for any real deployment), clean TAR only reaches 31%. The genuine and impostor LBPH distance distributions on wild LFW overlap heavily across the entire range — this is a distance-separability limit, not a threshold-tuning problem. Diagram 3 shows this.
