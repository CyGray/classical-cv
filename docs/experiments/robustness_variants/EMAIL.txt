Given these findings, should we proceed with writing this up as-is, or explore further? Below is a summary of the approaches I have already tested:

1. Write it up as-is: LBPH cannot effectively gate the LFW dataset, leading the cascade to rely on SFace, though latency remains acceptable. Even changing our thresholds does not help much (TAR up to 31%), indicating this is not a threshold issue.

2. Provide LBPH with more training photos per person: This improves results by approximately 9-10x, but the performance remains too low for practical deployment.

3. Test on a controlled dataset (AT&T faces, 40 people, uniform lighting): LBPH performs significantly better here (39% vs. 2% on LFW), demonstrating that the algorithm functions correctly but requires low-variation images.

4. Re-test the La Salle dataset with a single training photo per person: LBPH's performance drops sharply (8% vs. 90%+ with 10 photos). This suggests the primary limitation is the volume of quality training photos per person rather than the environment (controlled vs. wild).

I believe combining points 1, 3, and 4 presents the strongest narrative: LBPH's limitations stem from wild, single-photo enrollment rather than the test framework or threshold settings.
